A note on becoming.

I’m Jovan.

They say if you pretend long enough, the lie becomes you.

I’ve always liked that idea a little too much.

Perhaps it explains my long-standing habit of imagining myself as someone considerably more interesting, successful, powerful, or accomplished than I currently am. Somewhere along the way, the fantasy became more about the strange power of believing in a version of yourself before there's much evidence to support it.

I suppose that’s my version of manifestation. Convince yourself that the life you want is possible, then spend an unreasonable amount of time trying to make reality catch up.

My ambitions are, admittedly, ridiculously grandiose. I have dreams that are far too large for the head currently containing them, and an almost embarrassing determination to make an absurd amount of money someday. I don’t particularly want money for the sake of having a number in a bank account. I want the freedom, the scale, the ability to build things without constantly asking whether I can afford to.

Then there’s love, which is where all my rationality tends to go missing.

I’m something of a romantic, perhaps to an unreasonable degree. I have an almost utopian idea of love, and an embarrassing fondness for the possibility that some people are simply meant to find each other. Destiny is a dangerous concept to give someone with an imagination like mine.

I also have enough ego to keep things interesting. There is probably a little Icarus in me, forever looking upward and wondering why the sun should be the limit. I like to think it’s ambition. Occasionally, I’m pretty sure it’s narcissism with better branding.

Socially, I’m still a work in progress. I’m much better at conversations after they’ve already happened, when I can replay every sentence and invent seventeen better responses. I’m naturally introverted, and talking to strangers or standing in front of a room doesn’t always come as naturally as I’d like. I’m trying to change that. Public speaking, meeting people, saying what I actually think before the moment passes. Small things, perhaps, but they matter.

I don’t have the person I’ve imagined myself becoming figured out yet.

I’m still building him.
