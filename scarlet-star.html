SCARLET STAR

Prologue

Humanity once believed the world belonged to it.

Kings drew borders across maps. Armies fought over kingdoms. Empires rose, collapsed, and were rebuilt over their own ruins. For thousands of years, mankind looked upward and saw only an indifferent sky.

They were wrong.

Humanity was never alone.

The first one came without warning.

No prophecy announced his arrival. No heavenly choir descended from the clouds. No godly voice declared his name.

He was impossibly large, a creature that seemed less like an animal and more like a catastrophe given form. His wings blotted out the sun. His presence turned courage into panic, and wherever he passed, civilizations disappeared beneath ruin.

He did not conquer the world.

He simply destroyed it.

To him, mountains were obstacles, cities were markings in the dirt, and human lives possessed less significance than falling ash. He was young by the measure of creatures like him, yet within that innocence rested enough power to erase kingdoms.

Humanity called him a monster.

Later generations would call him something closer to a god.

And as mysteriously as he came, he vanished.

For a time, the world healed.

People began to believe it had all been some ancient nightmare.

Then, centuries later, another appeared.

Then another.

The forms changed.

So did the disasters.

Some came to conquer. Some came to rule. Some came seeking nothing more than destruction. They wore human faces, built armies, influenced kings, and moved through history like storms that happened to possess minds.

And the earth soon learned that a god did not need to descend from the heavens to become a calamity. They could walk among men, speak to kings, raise armies, and treat entire nations as pieces on a board. Whenever one appeared, history bent around them. Countries vanished, wars erupted, and cities were left in ruins before mankind once again gathered what remained and rebuilt.

They came one by one, separated by centuries and generations. No one knew when another would appear, what form they would take, or what they would seek. The only constant was the blood they left behind.

Across the centuries, scattered human records began using the same name.

The Scarlet Star.

The name was not born from the heavens, but from the earth beneath them. Wherever these beings appeared, the world seemed to turn red with war and death. By the time five had walked among mankind, the name had become a grim reminder that humanity was never the highest power in its own world.

Most people eventually forgot. Ancient rulers became names in history books, destroyed cities became ruins, and survivors turned the truth into myths about demons and gods. Scholars argued over whether the Scarlet Star had ever truly existed.

Humanity rebuilt, as it always did, and life continued.

But the cycle had never ended.

The Scarlet Star was still out there.
